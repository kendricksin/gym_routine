§ 06 · Where it Felt Like a Real Coach

The agent did coach work, not just bookkeeping.

Four moments from 28 days of chat logs where the AI did what you'd hope a smart coach would do — refuse a session for safety, name a pattern the lifter couldn't see yet, validate rest over guilt, push back on an unrealistic goal. Excerpts lightly condensed, intent preserved.

Moment 01 · Pattern recognition

It connected three drop sets across three exercises into one diagnosis.

Same session: I dropped weight mid-set on Bench, Arnold Press, and Pectoral Machine. Each one alone looked normal. The coach noticed them as a single signal.
→ User
I guess having to drop set means our form is getting off, either because we are too intense on our pacing between workouts or we are close to limit
← Coach Claw
You're reading the signal correctly. The pattern: Bench dropped 72.5 → 60. Arnold Press 16 → 14. Pectoral Machine 52.5 → 40. Fatigue is stacking across exercises — not just one muscle. RPE 9 on set 3-4 is appearing consistently. Drop sets = your body self-correcting before form breaks completely. You're approaching the limit of what you can recover from between sessions. That's not failure — it's data.
The skill in action: reading recent session history across exercises and synthesizing a single observation. This is what "the data is the source of truth" buys you — the coach can see what the lifter can't, because it has the full week in memory.
Moment 02 · No guilt trips

When I slept 12 hours and missed a session, it just said "good."

May 2 — Halfway through the program I crashed hard. Slept 12 hours, did nothing all morning. Most fitness apps would have pinged me with streak-loss anxiety.
→ User
I was unable to get anything done all morning just lying on my bed recovering
← Coach Claw
That's the debt collecting. Two weeks of 6-day training, 3,000kcal bulk, poor sleep quality — your body is demanding the recovery it needs. Lying in bed isn't wasted time if that's what it takes to keep going. Don't beat yourself up about it. Rest days are part of the program. The gym won't go anywhere.
What I expected vs what I got: I was prepared to be told to salvage an evening session. Instead the coach validated the rest, asked about bodyweight, and dropped the topic. The lack of guilt felt deliberate.
Moment 03 · Honest pushback

It pushed back on a grandiose HYROX goal — then revised when I gave better context.

May 3 — I asked if I could be ready for HYROX in August. The coach didn't just say yes. It mapped the gap, then updated its take when I clarified I wanted to finish, not podium.
→ User
do you think we can make it for hyrox august 15 with our current training objectives
← Coach Claw
Can you make it? Technically yes, if you commit fully. But you're currently doing powerlifting volume, HYROX needs conditioning + muscular endurance + cardio. The honest concern: You're sleeping 5 hours, struggling to eat enough, and 6 days/week lifting is already taxing. Adding HYROX-level conditioning on top might break you before the race. Do you want it? Because it's a different kind of hard.
→ User
i mean i dont really expect to perform exceptionally well, but just be able to complete
← Coach Claw
Fair point. Lightweight is an advantage in HYROX — less mass on the run, easier on burpees and wall balls. 120 minutes is a comfortable finishing time. Add 2 cardio sessions a week from July and you can comfortably finish in 1h50–2h10. The timeline is realistic for a finisher, not a podium.
What worked: the coach didn't immediately concede when I pushed back — it took the new information (lower ambition, lower body weight) and revised the assessment with specifics. Not stubborn, not pliable. Calibrated.
§ 07 · Where it Broke

The plumbing leaks, and the model can't add.

An honest report has to include the failure modes. Across 28 days the agent produced bad math, queried the wrong session for "last weight," had cron jobs fail silently, and let three database tables go stale for two weeks before I caught them.

Limit 01 · Bad arithmetic

It got a volume calculation visibly wrong, twice in one message.

May 12 — I asked the coach to compare tricep volume to last session. The first answer was off; the recalculation was also off. I had to flag it. The model writes to a database but has no calculator — basic math happens in tokens.
→ User
Math for volume seems off
← Coach Claw
You're right — let me recalculate. Last session: 27.5kg × 10 × 3 = 825, plus 30kg × 4 bonus = 120, total 945. This session: 27.5kg × 10 × 2 + 27.5kg × 9 = 797.5… Wait — that's not a drop, that's a +52.5kg increase. The arithmetic I showed was: 710 vs 860 → -150. That 860 was a misread + wrong number. Apologies.
Mitigation: volume math should be a deterministic skill that calls SUM over the workout-log table, not free-form LLM arithmetic. This is a fix on the roadmap, not a limitation of the approach.
Limit 02 · Wrong session retrieved

"Last session" for Wall Balls pulled from leg day instead of push day.

May 12 — Wall Balls are programmed differently on push days (light, high rep) vs leg days (heavy, race-prep). The coach grabbed the most recent log regardless of session type and prescribed 6kg when I'd just done 20kg the session before.
→ User
Wasnt our last wall ball yesterday with 20 x 3 sets? We should look into our session closing logic
← Coach Claw
You're right — I made an error. Last Wall Balls was 20kg × 20 × 3 on Session 18 (legs). The "last" for Wall Balls on push day was Session 16 (6kg × 25/15/15). The session-close logic issue: when I reference "last" for planning, I need to check the right session type's data, not just the most recent session regardless of type. I'll flag this for review.
The deeper issue: the exercise-history record is keyed on exercise name, not on (exercise, session_type). A fix means schema work, not prompt tweaking. The coach can't reason its way out of a missing column.
Limit 03 · Stale plumbing

Three database tables went unwritten for two weeks before I noticed.

May 12 — When I asked for a completeness review, the coach found that the progress, session_sequence, and exercise_history tables had all been written once during bootstrap and never updated. Daily logging only touched workout_log.
← Coach Claw, after diagnosis
Three real problems: SESSION_SEQUENCE never synced after Apr 29 — every session shows completed=False. PROGRESS table has only 3 records (Apr 18-20) — nothing logged since bootstrap. EXERCISE_HISTORY has 19 entries all with exercise_name: None. The actual training log is solid. But these gaps mean we can't do proper progression reports.
← Coach Claw, 5 min later
Housekeeping done ✅ 17 new PROGRESS records written. EXERCISE_HISTORY needs manual Lark edit (can't fix via API). Setting up weekly cron…
⚠️ Cron failed
Lesson: a chat-driven agent will happily keep operating on the one table that gets written every session, while everything downstream silently rots. The coach needs a "data audit" skill that runs on a schedule — and the cron itself needs to actually fire.
Limit 04 · Wrong reference

It used OHP benchmarks to grade Arnold Press, suggested I lift a "full grown woman".

May 3 — I asked how my lifts compared to standards. The coach used barbell Overhead Press numbers as the comparison even though I'd swapped to Arnold Press (lighter, dumbbell-based). The user-side push-back was funnier than the bug.
→ User
arnold press… are you serious? Thats lifting a full grown woman on each arm.
← Coach Claw
Haha, fair point — I pulled that number from OHP barbell standards, not Arnold Press. Wrong exercise comparison. Arnold Press reality check: 16–18kg per arm is the realistic intermediate target. That's a +4kg gap, not a +33kg gap. Much more achievable.
Pattern: the coach is strong on general fitness reasoning but its strength benchmarks come from training-data folklore, not exercise-specific reference tables. Either give it a real lookup tool or stop letting it produce numbers it can't source.
§ 08 · Under the Hood

What the coach actually is.

Not a chatbot wrapper. A set of seven skills running on OpenClaw, with Lark Base as the source-of-truth database and Lark chat as the interface. Every session log writes typed records to a real schema — and every coaching message above came out of reading those records. The underlying model is MiniMax 2.7.

SKILL · plan-session

"Heading to gym"

Reads the rotation, queues the next push/pull/legs day with target weights based on last session's RPE and rep performance.

SKILL · log-workout

Post-session dump

Parses freeform set logs ("Bench 70kg 7,7 rpe 8"), writes typed records to Lark Base, updates progression targets.

SKILL · suggest-deload

Stall detection

Catches consecutive RPE-10 sessions or drop-set patterns and proposes a lighter session before form breaks down.

SKILL · query-history

"How many days streak?"

Pulls PRs, trends, and rest-day counts. Also where most of the data-staleness issues surfaced — it could only see what was written.

SKILL · suggest-swap

Exercise substitutions

The Arnold Press vs OHP conversation, the Incline Fly → Pectoral Machine swap. Biomechanics reasoning + safety considerations.

SKILL · send-reminder

Nightly cron

Runs at 20:00. Closes stale sessions, sends morning briefings. Cron reliability turned out to be one of the weakest links.

The skills are open-source.

Steal the schema, fork the skills, run your own coach - works with any agent runtime that reads SKILL.md files.
